FSAE / Sensing

Tyre Temperature Monitoring System

Junior-led project with UC Motorsport (FSAE), building the team's first onboard tyre-temperature logging system. Tyre grip is temperature dependent, and the control system currently has no visibility into it.

Stage
Stage 1 of 2: a standalone proof-of-concept board
Built with
ESP32-S3, infrared sensor over I2C, KiCad, 3D-printed mounts
Power
Its own battery, no tie into the car's electrical system
Status
Built, and through its first round of track testing

01

Research

  • Compared how FSAE and F1 teams measure tyre temperature, and chose non-contact IR, the team's recommended approach.
  • Set a staged plan in the literature review: standalone first, then CAN into the ECU, with the architecture chosen now so Stage 2 is not a redesign.

02

Design

  • Picked the ESP32-S3 for its onboard CAN controller, needed for Stage 2.
  • Gave each of the three sensor connectors its own I2C bus, since every sensor ships at the same address. More sensors later is a population change, not a respin.
  • Caught before fabrication that the first microcontroller variant had no firmware image, and swapped to a footprint-compatible one with no redesign.
  • Stage 2 should now be simpler than drawn: the dash has an I2C input the sensors can hook straight into.

03

Build

  • Learned manual pick-and-place myself to assemble the board.
  • Developed the firmware for sensor reading, CSV logging and USB retrieval. It logs data correctly, with a few things still to optimise.

04

Mounting

  • Designed the housing to clip onto the car and take vibration and up to 2.5 G of lateral force.
  • Made rubber pads to sit between the clamp and the carbon aero, adding grip, damping vibration and protecting the carbon.
  • Moved the jacking bolts closer to the clamp's root after the first iteration pried itself open under load instead of clamping, and added wall loops and infill for stiffness.

05

Track testing

  • Held on with no issues through the first round of track testing.

First track test produced clean, usable data: 99.8% valid over a 46-minute session at 5 Hz. With all three sensors live, a cleared drive holds around 4 hours at that rate.

Learned

  • Check a part choice against every domain it affects. Here that meant confirming firmware support existed before locking in the microcontroller variant.
  • Gauge how far to design ahead. Extra sensor connectors were cheap, low-risk insurance for a stage that isn't scoped yet. A full CAN circuit would have been overreach, especially while I was still building PCB experience.
